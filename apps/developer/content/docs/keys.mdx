title: Keys and permissions
description: What a personal API key can reach, how to rotate it, and what stays limited to a signed-in session.
---

## How access works

A key belongs to the User who created it and acts as that User. When you create
it, you choose:

- **Organizations.** The key reaches only the Organizations you select. Joining
  another Organization later does not add it to the key.
- **Permissions.** _Read only_, or _Read and write_ for operations that change
  data.
- **Access to my data.** Personal data, such as your preferences and your own
  statistics. It is off by default and does not widen the Organization scope.

Every request is then limited by the intersection of the key's scope and what
you can do in Lootlog at that moment. If you lose a Discord role or leave an
Organization, the key loses that access too, while it keeps working in the
other Organizations it covers.

## Create and rotate keys

On the [API Keys](/keys) page you can hold up to 10 active keys. A key is valid
for 30, 90 or 365 days, or without expiration; the default is 90 days.

The secret is shown only once, right after you create the key. You can rename a
key later, but not change its scope. To change the scope or rotate a secret:

1. Create a new key with the scope you need.
2. Replace the secret in your integration and check that it works.
3. Delete the old key.

If a change succeeds but the key list fails to reload, select **Refresh list**.
This reloads the list without repeating the change. A new secret stays visible
while you retry, so save it before you leave the page.

## Revoke a key

A deleted or expired key stops authenticating new HTTP requests. An open
[realtime](/docs/realtime) connection loses its authorization within 60 seconds.

## Writes

A write needs a _Read and write_ key and your current permission for that
operation in the Organization. A key never manages your account, other keys or
Lootlog's internal operations.

Do not retry a write automatically after you lose its response: the request may
have succeeded. Read the resource first, then decide whether to repeat it.

## Personal data

With access to your data, a key reads and changes supported personal settings
such as your theme, appearance and toggles. Some settings decide which
Organizations receive your data, so they stay limited to a signed-in session:

- In `GET /users/@me/preferences`, `guildsOrder` and `hiddenGuildIds` list only
  Organizations of the key that you can still access. Changing them returns
  `403`.
- In `GET /users/@me/game-preferences/accounts/{id}`, detector rules for other
  Organizations are hidden and notification `guildIds` list only the key's
  Organizations. Changing `detector.routingRules` or notification `guildIds`
  returns `403`.
- Generic settings documents under `/preferences` and the `/timer-settings`
  operations need a session. Reading and writing timers themselves does not.
- System reservation-reminder rules are managed through reservations. A key
  cannot change them or the Discord target linked to them. Your own scheduled
  messages stay available.

Organization feeds and Ready Rooms under `/users/@me` follow the Organization
scope, not access to your data. A resource that spans several Organizations is
available only when the key covers all of them.

## Keep keys secret

Store keys in a secret manager or your server's environment. Never write them
to logs, error messages, URLs, userscripts or frontend code. Send a key only in
the `X-Api-Key` header: a request with an invalid key is rejected even when the
browser also has a Lootlog session.
